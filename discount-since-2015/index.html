What the measures mean

Discount rate is scholarships divided by gross tuition and fees for the whole college (CAS, Law and the Graduate School). Net at 45.9% average applies the FY2015 to FY2026 average discount to that year's gross tuition, holding enrollment fixed.

Dollars. FY2025 dollars use CPI-U, fiscal-year weighted, as on the other pages; FY2026 uses an estimated factor of 0.9739 (2026 CPI assumed 2.7% above 2025). First-year aid and price use calendar-year CPI-U for the fall the class entered, in 2025 dollars. Nominal shows each year's own dollars.

Grant aid per entrant is grant aid to the CAS first-time, full-time class divided by the class size: need-based grants (Common Data Set H2, lines e and k) plus institutional non-need grants to students without need (H2A, lines n and o). It includes federal and state need-based grants, which put it about 4% above IPEDS institutional grant aid, on average, for the fall 2015 to fall 2021 classes. Net price is list tuition less that aid, in 2025 dollars. Yield is enrolled divided by admitted.

How sensitivity was measured

For the fall 2015 to fall 2025 classes, log enrolled first-years were regressed on log real net price, log admits and a time trend. The price effect is 0.00 (standard error 0.56); yield on price gives a similar result. Real net price fell 44% while yield stayed between 9.7% and 15.4%.

This does not prove aid has no effect. Applications fell 21% over the same years, and if aid was raised to offset falling demand, the measured effect is pulled toward zero. The same method on fall 2001 to fall 2021 gave 0.64, used here as the conservative default.
