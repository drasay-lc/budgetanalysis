Closure and Distress Risk vs. Peers

Built with Claude Opus 5.5, November 2026, for Dan Rasay. The model code and full outputs are kept in a separate repository, drasay-lc/closure-risk. How the model did on real closures: Track Record.

This page applies the model from Kelchen, Ritter and Webber, "Predicting College Closures and Financial Distress" (Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia Working Paper 24-20, 2024). The model was trained on every private college that reported to IPEDS from 2002 to 2023, including about 1,840 that closed. Each college is scored on how closely its enrollment, staffing, revenue, expenses, balance sheet and the paper's four warning signs resemble those of colleges that went on to close. The scores rank colleges for screening; they are not forecasts.

How the model was built

IPEDS complete data files for the 2002 to 2023 collections (directory, fall enrollment, staff, private nonprofit and for-profit finance), combined at the six-digit OPEID level for private Title IV colleges. Dollars are in FY2023 terms, skewed levels are logged, values are winsorized at 2.5%, and features enter at two- to five-year lags, as in the paper.

Features follow the paper: enrollment, staff, revenue and expense levels, changes and shares; unrestricted net assets, plant debt and endowment; operating margin, days cash, EBIDA, debt to EBIDA and debt to assets; and four distress flags.

Where this differs from the paper

  • Closures are dated from IPEDS directory records rather than the Education Department's closed-school file, which could not be downloaded: about 1,840 private closures for 2004 to 2023, against the paper's 1,671 for 1996 to 2023, both peaking in 2016 to 2018.
  • Federal heightened cash monitoring, financial responsibility scores and county economic controls are not included. The paper finds county controls add little.
  • Training and test sets are split by college, which is stricter than splitting by year. The scenarios start from 2023 rather than the paper's 2019.

Reading the scores with care

  • Scores are correlational. A low score means a college looks little like those that closed, not that it faces no financial pressure.
  • The latest data are FY2023 finance and fall 2023 enrollment. Lewis & Clark's audited FY2024 to FY2026 results are not in the model.
  • The later-closure check uses news reports of closures announced after the data end; sources are listed in the model repository.
