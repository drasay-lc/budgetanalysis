How the peer groups were chosen

Willamette is in groups 1 and 2 and is listed once, in group 1.

Group 1: L&C comparison group

The institutions Lewis & Clark's Office of Institutional Research uses in its College of Arts and Sciences comparison reports: regional peers in Oregon and Washington plus national liberal arts colleges, taken as published without additions or removals.

Gettysburg, Gonzaga, Kalamazoo, Kenyon, Occidental, Reed, Rhodes, Sarah Lawrence, Skidmore, St. Olaf, Union, University of Portland, University of Puget Sound, Whitman, Willamette.

Group 2: Carnegie class

Private institutions that share Lewis & Clark's 2025 Carnegie classification, Mixed Undergraduate/Graduate-Doctorate, Small (a liberal arts college with law and graduate schools), or sit in the adjacent Medium band, with endowments closer to Lewis & Clark's than most of group 1, and with audited statements or federal single audit filings available.

Clark, Drew, Loyola New Orleans, Lynchburg, Pacific University, Seattle Pacific, Seattle University, Stetson, Willamette (also in group 1).

Group 3: Smaller endowment

Private institutions with a liberal arts core plus professional or graduate programs, endowments of roughly $70 million to $300 million at fiscal year 2025, and audited statements available.

Capital, Goucher, Hamline, John Carroll, Ohio Northern, Pacific Lutheran, Roger Williams, Saint Mary's College of California, University of La Verne, University of Redlands.

Source

Bachelor's degrees are award level 5, first major; summary rows are excluded. 1990 has no file.

The ten fields

  • Humanities: languages, English, history, philosophy and religion, area and ethnic studies, liberal arts and general studies. Arts: visual and performing arts.
  • Social sciences: economics, political science, international affairs, sociology, anthropology. Psychology on its own. Natural sciences and math: biology, chemistry, physics, math, environmental and natural resources.
  • Computer science includes engineering. Business, Health (nursing and health professions) and Communication (journalism and media). Other covers education, interdisciplinary codes, public administration, parks and recreation and the rest.

Reading with care

  • Sarah Lawrence reports every graduate under liberal arts, so it shows as all humanities.
  • Schools that grew professional programs (nursing at University of Portland and Seattle University, engineering at Gonzaga and Union) show falling humanities shares even when humanities counts held steady. Use the Graduates measure to check.
  • A few early years copy another year's figures (John Carroll, La Verne and Capital report identical 1987 and 1989 numbers; Redlands, Saint Mary's and Stetson repeat the year before in the mid-1980s), and La Verne put nearly every 1991 and 1992 graduate under one code. The 3-year average dampens these.
