How the peer groups were chosen

All three groups are available in the pickers. Willamette is in groups 1 and 2 and is listed once, in group 1.

Group 1: L&C comparison group

The institutions Lewis & Clark's Office of Institutional Research uses in its College of Arts and Sciences comparison reports: regional peers in Oregon and Washington plus national liberal arts colleges, taken as published without additions or removals.

Gettysburg, Gonzaga, Kalamazoo, Kenyon, Occidental, Reed, Rhodes, Sarah Lawrence, Skidmore, St. Olaf, Union, University of Portland, University of Puget Sound, Whitman, Willamette.

Group 2: Carnegie class

Private institutions that share Lewis & Clark's 2025 Carnegie classification, Mixed Undergraduate/Graduate-Doctorate, Small (a liberal arts college with law and graduate schools), or sit in the adjacent Medium band, with endowments closer to Lewis & Clark's than most of group 1, and with audited statements or federal single audit filings available.

Clark, Drew, Loyola New Orleans, Lynchburg, Pacific University, Seattle Pacific, Seattle University, Stetson, Willamette (also in group 1).

Group 3: Smaller endowment

Private institutions with a liberal arts core plus professional or graduate programs, endowments of roughly $70 million to $300 million at fiscal year 2025, and audited statements available.

Capital, Goucher, Hamline, John Carroll, Ohio Northern, Pacific Lutheran, Roger Williams, Saint Mary's College of California, University of La Verne, University of Redlands.

What the measures mean

Gross tuition is tuition and fees at list price for every student. Net tuition is what is left after institutional aid (scholarship allowances). The discount rate is aid divided by gross. Yield is the number of first-year students who enrolled divided by the number admitted, for the fall class that starts each fiscal year (FY2024 is the fall 2023 class).

Dollars are converted to FY2025 dollars with CPI-U, fiscal-year weighted, the same factors as the rest of the site.

Why these are IPEDS figures

  • Every school reports the same federal finance and admissions surveys each year, so the figures line up across all 34 schools. The audited-statement pages in this section cover fewer schools and years.
  • IPEDS tuition is college-wide: it includes graduate and law tuition where a school has them. Lewis & Clark's IPEDS figures match the discount spiral page (FY2024 net tuition $88.4M).
  • The latest year with finance data is FY2024; admissions data run one year further (fall 2024), shown in the CSV.

Reading yield with care

  • Yield falls when applications rise faster than enrollment, for example after joining the Common Application or going test-optional (Reed, Stetson and La Verne show such jumps). A falling yield with rising enrollment is not a weakening.
  • Net tuition growth can come from enrollment growth (more students), price, or a lower discount. Compare the gross line, the aid band and the discount chart to see which.
  • FY2021 reflects pandemic operations at most schools. Willamette's FY2022 and later include Pacific Northwest College of Art. Missing years (Lewis & Clark FY2011 and Lynchburg FY2019 admissions) are gaps.
