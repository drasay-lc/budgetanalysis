How the peer groups were chosen

All three groups are ranked together; the group filter only hides rows. Willamette is in groups 1 and 2 and is listed once, in group 1.

Group 1: L&C comparison group

The institutions Lewis & Clark's Office of Institutional Research uses in its College of Arts and Sciences comparison reports: regional peers in Oregon and Washington plus national liberal arts colleges, taken as published without additions or removals.

Gettysburg, Gonzaga, Kalamazoo, Kenyon, Occidental, Reed, Rhodes, Sarah Lawrence, Skidmore, St. Olaf, Union, University of Portland, University of Puget Sound, Whitman, Willamette.

Group 2: Carnegie class

Private institutions that share Lewis & Clark's 2025 Carnegie classification, Mixed Undergraduate/Graduate-Doctorate, Small (a liberal arts college with law and graduate schools), or sit in the adjacent Medium band, with endowments closer to Lewis & Clark's than most of group 1, and with audited statements or federal single audit filings available.

Clark, Drew, Loyola New Orleans, Lynchburg, Pacific University, Seattle Pacific, Seattle University, Stetson, Willamette (also in group 1).

Group 3: Smaller endowment

Private institutions with a liberal arts core plus professional or graduate programs, endowments of roughly $70 million to $300 million at fiscal year 2025, and audited statements available.

Capital, Goucher, Hamline, John Carroll, Ohio Northern, Pacific Lutheran, Roger Williams, Saint Mary's College of California, University of La Verne, University of Redlands.

How the ranking works

For each window, every school gets two measures: the percent change in net tuition after inflation (FY2025 dollars, CPI-U) and the change in yield in percentage points. Each measure is standardized across all 34 schools (distance from the average, in standard deviations) and the two are added, so a school must do well on both to rank high. The discount rate is left out on purpose.

What the measures are

  • Net tuition is tuition and fees after institutional aid, from the IPEDS finance survey. It is college-wide, so it includes graduate and law tuition where a school has them.
  • Yield is first-year students enrolled divided by those admitted, from the IPEDS admissions survey. FY2024 is the fall 2023 entering class.
  • FY2024 is the latest year with finance data for every school.

Cautions

  • Yield can fall simply because applications rise faster than enrollment (Reed's applications rose about three quarters), and rise because the applicant pool shrinks (Willamette). The applicant and first-year class columns show which.
  • Net tuition can grow through enrollment, price or a lower discount; this page does not separate them. Willamette's figures include Pacific Northwest College of Art from FY2022.
  • FY2017 to FY2019 starting points avoid the pandemic years, but FY2021 sits inside every window.
