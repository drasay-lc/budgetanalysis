Which schools are included

All schools are ranked together; the rank band buttons only hide rows.

The set

Every college ranked ahead of Lewis & Clark in the U.S. News & World Report 2027 Best National Liberal Arts Colleges list, published September 22, 2026. Lewis & Clark is tied at No. 97, so the set is ranks 1 to 96 (ties share a number).

The bands follow the U.S. News rank: Top 25 (ranks 1 to 25), 26 to 50, and 51 to 96.

Left out of the ranking

Four schools report no meaningful tuition revenue, so a change in net tuition cannot be measured. They stay in the CSV with their admissions figures.

United States Naval Academy, United States Military Academy, United States Air Force Academy (no tuition), and Berea College (charges no tuition).

Kept, with care

Two schools have very small net tuition, so modest dollar changes become large percentages and pull them to the bottom of the net tuition measure.

Principia College (about $0.8 million in FY2024, in FY2025 dollars) and Soka University of America (about $1.2 million).

How the ranking works

For each window, every school gets two measures: the percent change in net tuition after inflation (FY2025 dollars, CPI-U) and the change in yield in percentage points. Each measure is standardized across all ranked schools (distance from the average, in standard deviations) and the two are added, so a school must do well on both to rank high. The discount rate is left out on purpose. The method matches the peer group page, so ranks on the two pages can be read the same way, though they rank different sets.

What the measures are

  • Net tuition is tuition and fees after institutional aid, from the IPEDS finance survey. It is college-wide, so it includes graduate and law tuition where a school has them. Lewis & Clark's includes its law school and graduate school, which most schools in this set do not have.
  • Yield is first-year students enrolled divided by those admitted, from the IPEDS admissions survey. FY2024 is the fall 2023 entering class.
  • FY2024 is the latest year with finance data for every school.

Cautions

  • Yield can fall simply because applications rise faster than enrollment, and rise because the applicant pool shrinks. Schools that fill much of the class through early decision report high yields. The applicant and first-year class columns show which way the counts moved.
  • Net tuition can grow through enrollment, price or a lower discount; this page does not separate them.
  • The U.S. News rank is from the 2027 edition, while the measures end in FY2024, so the rank describes where schools stand now, not where they started.
  • FY2017 to FY2019 starting points avoid the pandemic years, but FY2021 sits inside every window.
